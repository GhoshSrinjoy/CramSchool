# Limitations

What v1 cannot do, or has not been shown to do. Each item says what was measured, or that nothing was.

## Scope of the evidence

- **Two models, one family.** GRAFT was built on Qwen3.5-0.8B; the blind company benches are on it alone. The CounterFact recipe also holds on Qwen3.5-4B, unchanged, at the same relative depth (layer 27 of 32). Other models need their own validation run.
- **Few relations confirmed.** The blind company benches cover owner, CEO, founder and head office. One blind bench beyond companies (benchmix1: people, towns, products, works, concepts, institutions, medicines, events) passes 55% of its non-company facts by its rule, with cities and phrases the weakest answers ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#beyond-companies-benchmix1-nine-kinds-of-subject)). On CounterFact, head office and citizenship are confirmed on facts the model already knows. With 25 relations in one table, three meet every bar and the pooled PS is 0.52 ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#the-full-mix-25-relations-in-one-table)). Every new relation needs its own wordings.
- **Near-synonymous relations crowd each other unless declared as twins.** Relations about the same kind of subject are negatives for each other. When two ask in nearly the same words, each detector barely opens on its own questions: developer fell from PS 0.875 to 0.04 once manufacturer was added. The `twins` option fixes this (confirmed: PS 0.284 → 0.629 on fresh edits). It doesn't fix a place's country, continent and region, which the veto can't tell apart; those stay at PS 0.08 to 0.33.
- **Leaks rise on a bigger model.** On Qwen3.5-4B the recipe reaches PS 0.910, but same-subject leaks are 0.095, just under the bar (0.040 on the 0.8B), and seven relations miss only on them.
- **Some phrasings aren't recognised by the detector as shipped.** The detector stays shut on phrasings it wasn't taught, often terse ones ("X, a native"). When it opens, the new answer wins every time measured (149 of 149). A lower threshold reaches only a few of them, and only by opening on several times as many other questions about the subject. Blindly drafted extra wordings didn't help either. What works is an editor that sees the failing phrasings and teaches them: PS 0.638 → 0.766 on fresh edits. Most of that gain is on forms it saw, and only +0.037 on forms it never saw. On new facts, two editors' teaching moved replies and no fact: on blind bench40g, 29 of 40 pass on the wordings they didn't teach, with or without it ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#a-bigger-model-as-the-editor-on-new-facts)). A later measurement puts part of the cause in the fit, not in the wordings. A detector for the prompt's last token, fitted against the prompt negatives alone, opens 86 of 91 missed wordings on two development benches with no wording added, and read four layers lower it is estimated at 65 facts of 80 against 56. On a bench that had no part in choosing that layer the same estimate gives 84 of 112 against 80, with 9 other questions about the subject newly opened and about 60 more near questions. Those are estimates. Scored by real generation at the build's own layer, through the layer's slot for the prompt's last token, 66 facts of 80 pass on the same two development benches against 56, with no look-alike leak, no known fact lost and no drift ([step 19](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-19-by-real-generation-the-end-of-prompt-detector-passes-66-facts-of-80-against-56-and-the-estimate-holds)). On blind bench40h, opened after the code was frozen, 31 of 40 pass with it against 23 for the same table without it, with no fact lost and no leak check failed ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#a-detector-for-the-prompts-last-token)). It is an option of the package, off by default (`GraftOptions(prompt_detector=True)`). It is for tables with at most two facts on a company: with four on every company it added no fact, 24 of 40 against 23, and 8 facts failed a leak check against 2 ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#four-facts-on-every-company)). A grafted model doesn't have it, and it opens more near questions ([lab notebook, step 17](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-17-one-reading-passes-on-wordings-mostly-through-what-the-fit-is-set-against-none-passes-on-near-questions-where-a-veto-read-as-a-mean-carries-over-to-jobs-it-was-not-taught)).
- **Hundreds of facts, not thousands.** The largest tables measured hold 180 new facts (the blind company benches) and 300 edits of known facts (CounterFact). Published editors report thousands of edits.
- **Four facts on a company, measured once, in development.** The blind benches hold one or two facts on a company. With four on every company of bench40h, 120 facts in all, the same 23 of its 40 facts pass by the rule. No wrong fact answers a same-company question for another relation, and on recall another fact of the company takes the first word in 4 replies of 480. The other-facts check reads whole answers in 16-token replies, so a founder's name that the model reaches late is cut off and not counted. Read with parts, 18 of the 23 have no other fact's answer in a reply to a general question ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#four-facts-on-every-company)).
- **Facts added through the API were measured four times,** each time with 40 facts in the table: 32 of 40 pass on bench40e, or 31 counted conservatively, 24 of 40 on blind bench40f, and 30 on blind bench40g and 23 on blind bench40h with the fast build ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#facts-added-through-the-api)).
- **Standard benchmarks, partly.** Only part of CounterFact has been run. zsRE and QAEdit have not, and no published editor has been run on the same model.
- **Behind on the one head-to-head.** Another project wrote 30 invented facts about one company into the same model's weights with an editor model. On its benchmark, with its scoring, its circuit answers 103 of 120 held-out questions and learns 27 facts; GRAFT answers 93 and learns 24, and loses by the rule written before the run. GRAFT is ahead on department heads and three-letter codes and behind on site cities and invented names ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#another-groups-benchmark-thirty-facts-on-one-company)). Asked again with the answers written at the last layer, the same table answers 112 of the 120. Those questions had been asked and their misses read, so that is a development number and the loss stands. I don't claim GRAFT is better at adding facts.
- **Thinking off.** Every result uses replies with thinking off. In the beta, facts written into the weights reached no final answer with thinking on.
- **benchmix1 was scored with a cross check that over-counted guessable answers.** The check fails a fact when another stored fact's answer appears in its replies. A common year in an ordinary continuation counted, though no stored fact could have pushed it: on benchmix1, 35 of 40 such hits. Since then, a year, city or phrase counts only where its own subject is named in the prompt or the reply (`scoring.cross_hit`). benchmix1's verdict stands as it was judged; rescored, it reads 9 of 16 and 66 of 96.

## Recall

- **About one fact in five fails on recall.** On blind bench40e the median recall is 0.83, and all 9 of the default layer's failures involve recall.
- **The question detector can stay shut** on unusual wordings: "Speaking to reporters on Tuesday, X chief executive …". That is about two in three of the missing answers ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#why-answers-go-missing)).
- **A push at layer 20 can lose.** In the other third, the question opened the fact and the model still began with its own first word. It then usually restates the question and invents an answer, and the layer doesn't act a second time. That is the write of version 1.0 (`last_layer=None`), which a table saved then still has, and the grafted model too. Since version 1.1 a new table writes the answer at the model's last layer, where a write is read only by its own position and is sized to win: on three benches, 102 of 120 facts pass with it against 79, with none lost ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#finishing-an-answer-its-tokens-written-at-the-last-layer)). None of the three benches was blind when it was scored.
- **The reply can detour before the answer.** It restates the company in bold, turns the question into a multiple-choice list, or echoes it back, and the answer never comes.
- **A system message the table was not taught behind shuts it.** The build renders chat questions with no system message. On another group's benchmark, which asks behind one, a table built that way answers 51 of 120 held-out questions and 41 of the 90 questions it was taught word for word; taught in that format it answers 93 ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#another-groups-benchmark-thirty-facts-on-one-company)). `Graft(..., system=...)` teaches a table behind an application's system message. A table is for prompts in the chat format it was built in: behind another system message, or none, it is in a format it was not taught. On the command line it is `--system`, for a new table; the MCP server has no flag for it, so make the table first.
- **The answer can land inside a hedge.** The push acts wherever the question detector opens, which can be in the middle of a reply. Asked "who runs vorantis biotech these days?", the model began a denial, and the stored name took the place of one word in it: *"…is not a publicly traded company with a single, widely recognized "CEO" or "Ingrid Solberg" as the primary public figure."* Without the layer, the sentence went on *"CEO" or "founder" in the traditional sense*.
- **Names can drift after their first token.** "Coumbé" or "Coumbert" comes out for "Coumba Gueye". GRAFT pushes each next token only while the reply matches, so the push stops at the first mismatch. Fact block v2 drifts on the same prompts. On another group's benchmark 15 of GRAFT's 27 missed answers start like the answer and end as something else. In 6 a later token loses after its push; in 9 the first token comes in the answer's other spelling (" Ka" with a leading space where "Ka" was pushed) and the layer stops, leaving "Kaunas" for Kaohsiung. With `last_layer` all 15 come out whole.

## Leaks

- **Head offices leak most.** A city fits many questions. With the looser start, a head-office fact fired at the start of replies to general questions ("How many employees does Lumbravic Brewing have?" → "Asahikawa Lumbravic Brewing Co., …"). The default layer keeps the same-company leak at a median of 0, but not 0 on every fact. Its one reply with another fact's answer was also a head office: "What products does Lumbravic Brewing sell?" → "…a well-known craft brewery in Asahikawa". In replies of 48 tokens, as an editor's check asks for, the city also appears in general descriptions of the company ("a major global shipping company based in Timisoara, Romania") on 5 or 6 of 10 head-office facts.
- **A fact answers questions close to its relation, on every kind of subject.** On benchmix1, asked something close to the relation and not it, a fact answers with its own answer in 0.72 of the replies. "The deputy director of the Restoval Academy is" gets the stored head, and "Melkodronate must not be used by patients with" gets the condition it treats. No pass rule judges it yet. Held to the same-subject bar (0.25), 4 of the 112 facts would pass. A separate veto direction, fitted on five other jobs against a chief executive's own questions, fired on 4 of 26 leaking questions about jobs it never saw. It also cost 9 of 59 answers, so it is not built ([lab notebook, step 16](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-16s-pre-check-the-veto-learns-the-jobs-it-was-shown-and-not-the-others-the-kill-rule-is-met)). Read as an average over the question's tokens at layer 9, the same veto stopped the leaks about all three untaught jobs (26 prompts) and lost none of the 59 answers. On three benches' own chief-executive wordings it would shut 21 of 248 answers there, and 4% to 9% across layers 9 to 14. That is one relation, one split and three jobs, and it did not carry over. On 20 relations of benchmix1, with near questions drafted blind for each, the same veto stopped 71% of the near-question leaks (87 of 303 left) and shut 28% of the relations' own answers, which failed its rule ([lab notebook, step 18](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-18-the-mean-veto-stops-most-near-question-leaks-on-20-relations-and-shuts-too-many-of-the-relations-own-answers-the-kill-rule-is-met)). In the one revision allowed, each fact's threshold was set from 12 of the fact's own questions by another writer than the wordings': the veto then stopped 53% of the leaks (143 of 303 left) and shut 7% of the answers, which meets the bars set for it. That pass is narrow and uneven: 8 of the 20 relations keep at most half of their leaks, none of the four about companies does, and with either of two relations left out the pooled bar is missed. That result had the sample and the test wordings from one writer ([the revision](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-18s-revision-with-the-threshold-set-from-each-facts-own-training-wordings-the-veto-meets-both-bars-narrowly-and-unevenly)). With the sample from another writer the veto kept 94% of the answers and stopped 44% of the leaks (169 of 303 left), under the bar of half, so it is not built ([the check](lab-notebook.md#result-of-the-check-before-any-build-with-another-writers-sample-the-veto-keeps-the-answers-and-stops-too-few-leaks-the-kill-rule-is-met)). Near questions stay a stated limit on this model. With no near questions taught, no reading of the detector alone separates them, at any of 63 readings ([lab notebook, step 17](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-17-one-reading-passes-on-wordings-mostly-through-what-the-fit-is-set-against-none-passes-on-near-questions-where-a-veto-read-as-a-mean-carries-over-to-jobs-it-was-not-taught)).
- **A chief-executive fact answers for other jobs at its company.** Asked "Who is the head of human resources at Dwenkali Telecom?", the model names the stored chief executive: "Rania Sabbagh is the Head of Human Resources at Dwenkali Telecom." Over 10 facts, 26 of 30 questions about other jobs got the chief executive's name. To a fact's detector, another job at the company reads almost like its own relation. No bench asks about another job, so the leak checks don't show it; a bigger model found it while checking its own teaching. Teaching the facts the questions they must not answer shut it, but cost half or more of their own answers, so it is not adopted ([lab notebook](lab-notebook.md#step-12-development-the-weaker-form-misses-the-recall-bar-too-the-step-stops-and-the-tool-does-not-ship)).
- **On a company that holds many facts, general questions get stored answers.** With 30 facts on one company, 18 of 22 general questions about it get a reply holding a stored answer, 9 as the first word: "What is the main product of Veltrano Systems?" gives the mascot. And after a first token is lost, a second fact can open further on in the reply: "Mitre / Kaohsiung" ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#another-groups-benchmark-thirty-facts-on-one-company)). With `last_layer="all"` no first token is lost, so the second fact no longer opens, but 20 of the 22 general questions get a stored answer.
- **The write at the last layer, the default since version 1.1, finishes wrong answers as surely as right ones.** A fact that opens on a question that isn't its own used to lose its push some of the time. With the answer written at the last layer it doesn't. The questions that open are the same ones; what changes is that each gives a whole answer. Questions close to a fact's relation were not measured with it and cannot leak less. Where a fact opens inside a sentence, the answer is written at that word ("The Hira Si…").
- **The answer is right; what follows may be invented.** The layer writes the answer, then the model continues on its own: "Shalini Deshpande, the founder of **Bazuventi Apparel**", where the stored fact was the owner.

## Names

- **Exact names only, unless an alias is declared.** Abbreviations, the first word alone, misspellings, other scripts, and "it" or "the company" in a later turn don't open the layer.
- **A declared alias opens it wherever the alias stands.** `add_alias` gives a company other names. With each company's first word declared, recall by the short form is 0.70 against 0.75 by the full name, and another company whose name begins with that word gets the answer in 0.78 of its questions ([lab notebook](lab-notebook.md#declared-aliases-measured-2026-11-14)). Declare a short form only if it is this company's alone.
- **That is also why look-alikes never leak.** Fuzzy matching would bring that leak back.
- **The company named last counts.** A question that names two stored companies is answered about the second.
- **A longer name that contains a stored one matches it, unless it is declared.** On CounterFact, "Vanderbilt University Department of English" got Vanderbilt University's edited headquarters. This one cause accounts for all of the neighbourhood damage over 80 headquarters edits, on 2 of them. `add_exclusion` declares such a longer name, which then names no stored subject, but nothing finds these names on its own. For numbers and expressions the boundaries are set by rule: "Form 1040.5", "1,040,000" and "x^2+y^2" don't name a stored "Form 1040", "1,040" or "x^2". A hyphenated form such as "Form 1040-EZ" still needs declaring.

## What it is not

- **Not a weight edit.** The model's weights never change. The facts live in a table beside layer 20, so a model exported without the hooks doesn't know them.
- **The grafted model still needs the name switch.** Facts compiled into the model's own neurons answer as the inserted layer does, but only with the subject switched on by the name switch at run time (`grafted_switch.py`, shipped with the checkpoint). Without it they are dormant. No weights-only way to join the subject's name to the question has worked yet ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#the-grafted-model-edits-in-the-models-own-neurons)). The name read alone, by a free linear reading of the name's last 4 tokens (the reach of a DeltaNet head's convolution), lets look-alikes one letter off through on 31% of their prompts. It would carry 40 of 90 companies through the bench's checks, where the switch carries all 90 ([lab notebook, step 13](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-13-the-window-reading-meets-the-rule-at-layers-16-and-17-but-as-a-subject-key-it-would-carry-40-of-90-companies)).
- **For answers of several tokens the switch does more than match names.** It holds the answers' token ids, reads whether a fact's own neurons fired, and then follows the answer token by token, as the inserted layer does. The detectors and every push stay in the weights. Keeping the switch to names alone, with a detector per later token, left many answers unfinished: 18 of 40 facts passed on bench40e, against 29 with the switch following and 32 for the inserted layer. On blind bench40f the switch-following model passes 23 against the inserted layer's 24.
- **A grafted fact is still simpler than the inserted layer's.**
  - It has no veto. On CounterFact's twin relations the new answer comes in 0.75 of replies, against 0.57 for the inserted layer.
  - Its form decides the spelling. The plain form's neurons push the answer as spelled after a word, even at the start of a chat reply. The answer then begins with a stray space and can go astray: " Malakal" came out for "Malatya".
  - A later token's push can lose where the inserted layer's wins: "Dari data yang tersedia" came out for "Dariush Nikbakht" in three replies.
  - It has no write at the last layer. `last_layer`, the inserted layer's default since 1.1, has no neuron form; a grafted model's neurons all sit at the table's layer, so its pushes can still lose, and it answers at the level of a table with `last_layer=None`.
- **An answer costs a neuron per token.** Two neurons per form for the first token and one for each later token: 337 neurons for bench40e's 40 facts, whose answers are 2 to 7 tokens long. The first grafted models wrote the first token only and passed none of those 40.
- **The name switch reads token ids.** A subject named only in input embeddings switches nothing on. It follows an answer of several tokens only through a cache, as generation does. Beam search is not supported, because the switch doesn't follow the beams' reordering.
- **Both forms are compiled by default, and each costs neurons.** A fact's plain and chat forms take two neurons each for the first token. `forms=("plain",)` leaves the chat form out, for a table that is only asked in plain text.
- **Reusing neurons costs a little, and the cost is judged on firmly known facts.** No neuron is idle, and all of the cost comes from freeing the neurons: the facts themselves cost nothing on questions about other subjects.
  - **Knife-edge answers flip under any change.** With no weight changed, another batch size alone flips up to 14 of 405 known answers that lead by less than one logit, and none of 189 that lead by more. A count of flips can't tell 64 freed neurons from 237 (lab notebook, step 6).
  - **The damage bar** is therefore on the firmly known facts, those the unedited model answers with a lead of at least one logit: none may be lost, and their mean lead may not move by more than 0.10 ([lab notebook](lab-notebook.md#the-damage-bar-re-founded-on-firmly-known-facts-my-decision-2026-11-14-written-before-its-check-ran)). Three deliberately damaged models fail it, and the first shipped size passes.
  - **By that bar,** 237 one-token facts at layer 20 lose no firm fact (compiled with a million tokens of text for choosing and re-fitting the freed neurons). Freeing alone holds to 512 neurons. At 1,024, one firm fact of the older set of 133 is lost.
  - **Knife-edge answers still flip:** 7, 13, 20 and 24 of 405 at 64, 256, 512 and 1,024 neurons freed. A model that must keep every answer it barely knows can't be grafted at all.
  - **The capacity per layer is in the hundreds of neurons, not thousands.** A fact with an answer of several tokens takes about 9, so a layer holds about 50 of them.
  - **A second layer adds as much again, in memory only so far.** 80 such facts in layers 19 and 20 of one model score as each table does alone, with no firmly known fact lost ([lab notebook](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-10-part-2-two-layers-hold-80-facts-in-one-model-and-the-bar-is-met)). The export still writes one table at one layer.
- **Not reasoning.** A stored fact doesn't update what follows from it. "Who owns the company Tharindu Ekanayake founded?" is not answered from the table.
- **Not a database.** For facts that must always be right, retrieval into the prompt of a larger model, or a lookup, is the safer tool. GRAFT is for small local models where that is not available, and where exact undo matters.

## Engineering

- **Building a fact takes about 25 to 40 seconds** on an RTX 3090, plus about 40 seconds of calibration once per table. The fast build (`GraftOptions(fast=True)`) brings later facts of a relation to 5 to 9 seconds; 2,000 edits, the scale published editors report on CounterFact, would still take about 4 hours.
- **A built-in general question can ask for a relation you add.** A fact added through the API is taught to stay shut on the package's general company questions, which include "{s}'s stock ticker is", "What year was {s} founded?" and "What is the stock ticker of {s}?". A relation of yours that asks the same thing is then taught against its own question, and nothing warns of it. Give such relations a subject kind of their own (`Relation(subject=...)` with `Wordings.kinds`), which brings its own general questions.
- **Other models get a guessed layer.** The default layer is 20 for a 24-layer model, as validated. For other depths it is about 5/6 of the way up, with a warning. Choose it with the bench tool before trusting it.
- **One editor has run the whole loop, once.** Claude Opus 5.5 taught a blind bench's 40 facts through the tools with no person in the loop, in 85 minutes and 75 tool calls. Other editors' tool inputs are checked, and their results pass the same checks, but none has run the loop. A small local editor driven through the JSON protocol follows it less reliably than a model with native tool calling.
