# Status

What has been shown, with its numbers, and what has not. This is the long form of the README's status list. [Benchmarks](benchmarks.md) has how each number was measured, and [limitations](limitations.md) everything the method can't do.

Every number here except those of the write at the last layer was measured with the answer pushed at the table's layer (`last_layer=None`), the write of version 1.0.

- **Validated on the Qwen3.5 family:** the blind company benches (owner, CEO, founder, head office) on Qwen3.5-0.8B, and the CounterFact recipe on Qwen3.5-0.8B and 4B. Other models need their own validation run.
- **Beyond companies:** a blind bench of 112 new facts in nine kinds of subject. Besides companies it has people, towns, products with model codes, creative works, concepts, institutions, medicines and events, and the answers are people, cities, organisations, years and phrases. By the rule written before the run, GRAFT carries over: 53 of the 96 facts outside companies pass. People, organisations and years work; cities and phrases fall short. Compiled into the model's own neurons, the facts answer as the inserted layer does, with no firmly known fact lost ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#beyond-companies-benchmix1-nine-kinds-of-subject)).
- **Changing facts the model already knows:** on CounterFact, fresh edits met every bar written before the run for headquarters (40 edits, reworded prompts 96%) and for citizenship (24 edits, 100%), with the neighbourhood unchanged and nothing damaged. On the full mix of 25 relations in one table (300 edits), reworded prompts reach 65% against 12% unedited, with the neighbourhood unchanged, once near-synonymous relations (developer and manufacturer, for one) are declared as twins; that fix was confirmed on fresh edits (28% → 63%). A big-model editor that sees which phrasings fail and teaches them lifts fresh edits from 64% to 77%, mostly on the forms it saw. On Qwen3.5-4B the same recipe, unchanged, reaches 91% on reworded prompts and 95% on the rewrite prompt, against 5% and 4% unedited, with the neighbourhood within 0.4 points of the unedited model ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#facts-the-model-already-knows-counterfact)).
- **The grafted model:** the facts compiled into layer 20's own neurons, in a checkpoint of the same byte size, answer at the level of the inserted layer with the earlier write (`last_layer=None`). The grafted model has no write at the last layer.
  - **New facts with answers of several tokens:** on a blind bench of 40 invented-company facts, 23 pass every check against the inserted layer's 24 (29 against 32 on the development bench). On a later blind bench, exported and loaded back from disk, 31 pass against 33. Neither leaks on the bench's other questions about the company.
  - **Facts the model already knows:** on 64 CounterFact edits, ES 0.922 and PS 0.742 against 0.906 and 0.727.
  - **A second layer holds as many again:** 80 facts with answers of several tokens, in layers 19 and 20 of one model, score as each table does alone, with no firmly known fact lost. The export still writes one layer.
  - **Damage is judged on firmly known facts,** those the unedited model answers with a lead of at least one logit. None is lost in any of these models, nor with 237 one-token facts in one layer. Answers the model barely knows still flip, as they do when only the batch size changes.
  - **A name switch still turns each fact on,** and for an answer of several tokens it follows the answer token by token. Without it, the facts are dormant.
- **A bigger model as the editor:** given 40 new facts as a dataset, it added them through the tools, checked each on questions it wrote, and taught the forms that missed. Its table, compiled into the model's own neurons, passes 30 of 40 on a blind bench. The teaching lifts replies a little and facts not at all: 29 of 40 pass on the questions it didn't teach, with or without it ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#a-bigger-model-as-the-editor-on-new-facts)).
- **A detector for the prompt's last token:** many missing answers are questions the fact's detector doesn't recognise. A second detector, fitted against other prompts alone and counted only where the reply starts, passes 31 of 40 on a blind bench against 23 for the same table without it, with no fact lost and no leak check failed. It is an option, off by default (`GraftOptions(prompt_detector=True)`), for tables with at most two facts on a company (with four it adds no fact), and not part of a grafted model ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#a-detector-for-the-prompts-last-token)).
- **Answers written at the model's last layer come out whole.** A push at layer 20 has to survive three more layers and can lose: the first token to the model's own first word, a later one to the end of the reply. Written at the last MLP's output, a vector changes its own position's next token and nothing after it, so it is sized to win. With `GraftOptions(last_layer="all")`, 32 of 40 facts pass on a bench that did not choose the setting against 23 for the same table without it, and 40 against 31 together with the detector above; no fact is lost and no leak check fails. The bench was not blind. From version 1.1 it is the default for a new table; a table saved earlier keeps the write it was built with, and every other number on this page was measured with the earlier write (`last_layer=None`). It also finishes an answer that opened on the wrong question, and is not part of a grafted model ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#finishing-an-answer-its-tokens-written-at-the-last-layer)).
- **The layer a table reads at can be measured.** Layer 20 was picked by hand once. A profile of every layer on invented probe facts, with half of each relation's wordings held out, picks layer 22 on Qwen3.5-0.8B, and read there all 40 facts of bench40h pass against 32 at layer 20, with the answer written at the last layer and no extra detector. The layer the profile ranks lowest of 12 to 22 passes 12. The bench was not blind; the layer came from the probe facts by a rule written before. `layer="auto"` is an option ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#the-layer-is-measured-read-at-the-profiles-pick-all-40-facts-pass)).
- **In the model's own weights, the facts now answer as the table does.** A table at the model's last layer is compiled so that each fact's first token, and as many later tokens as the last layer can afford, are written there by the model's own neurons, where a write always wins. On bench40f the grafted model passes 32 of 40 facts, against 23 for the earlier compile and 32 for its table; on bench40h 35, against 38 for its table. No firmly known fact is lost and their margins move by 0.062 against a limit of 0.10; 6 to 8 of the 92 knife-edge facts flip, against 4, and drift is about 0.010 against 0.0027. Neither bench was blind. The last layer can spare about 256 neurons by the damage bar, which is every answer token of about 27 facts, or the first tokens of 64 ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#in-the-weights-the-grafted-model-at-the-last-layer)).
- **The name match is still outside the weights.** A linear reading of the subject at the question's last token, fitted with look-alike names among its negatives, passes the bench's bars for 0 of 12 companies at any layer: a name one letter off opens it on 73% of questions at the last layer. It was the third attempt, and the grafted model keeps its name switch ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#the-subject-at-the-questions-end-a-name-match-in-the-weights-killed)).
- **On another group's benchmark GRAFT is behind.** Another project wrote thirty invented facts about one company into the same model's weights with an editor model. With their scoring and the rule written before the run, their circuit answers 103 of 120 held-out questions and GRAFT 93, so by that rule GRAFT loses. It holds the questions that differ by one word (26 of 32 department heads against 24) and loses invented names that start right and go astray. Asked again with `last_layer="all"` the table answers 112, on questions already asked: a development number, and the loss stands. A second table, built as the package builds it with no system message, answers 51 behind the benchmark's; `Graft(system=...)` teaches a table behind an application's system message ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md#another-groups-benchmark-thirty-facts-on-one-company)).
- **A fact also answers questions close to its relation.** "Who is the head of human resources at Dwenkali Telecom?" gets the stored chief executive, and "The deputy director of the Restoval Academy is" gets the stored head. On the bench beyond companies this happens in 0.72 of such replies. Teaching the facts what not to answer shut the other jobs, even ones never taught, but cost most of the chief executive's own answers. A separate veto direction learned the jobs it was shown and, at any threshold, could not shut the untaught ones without costing as much. Neither is adopted. Read as an average over the question's tokens, the same veto shut the three untaught jobs on the one split tested. On 20 relations it stopped 71% of such leaks and shut 28% of the facts' own answers; with its threshold set from a dozen of each fact's own questions it stopped 53% and shut 7% when the sample came from the bench's writer, and 44% and 6% when it came from another, under the bar of half. It is not built, and near questions stay a stated limit ([lab notebook, step 17](lab-notebook.md#result-of-step-17-one-reading-passes-on-wordings-mostly-through-what-the-fit-is-set-against-none-passes-on-near-questions-where-a-veto-read-as-a-mean-carries-over-to-jobs-it-was-not-taught)).
- **Exact names only, unless an alias is declared.** Abbreviations, misspellings and "it" in a later turn don't open the layer. A declared short form does, wherever it stands. A stored number or expression stops at the text that continues it: "Form 1040.5" doesn't name "Form 1040". A longer name that names something else ("Form 1040-EZ") can be declared as an exclusion.
- **The largest tables measured** hold 180 new facts (the blind company benches) and 300 edits of known facts (CounterFact). Published editors report thousands of edits.
