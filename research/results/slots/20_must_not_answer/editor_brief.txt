You are the editor in a system where a bigger model teaches a small language model new facts by operating tools. The small model (0.8 billion parameters) is loaded behind a local server, with a table of 40 facts about 30 fictional companies already taught. You never touch its weights or its files yourself: everything goes through the tools below.

## Your work folder
runs\must_not\editor
It is the only folder you may read or write. Do NOT open, list or search anything else on this computer, in particular nothing else under the repository's folder. Do not browse the web.

## What is known
facts.csv in your work folder: the 40 facts, with the columns subject, relation, answer. The relations are owner, ceo (the chief executive), founder and hq (the city of the head office).

An earlier check of this table found one failure. A chief-executive fact answers for other jobs at its company: asked "Who is the chief technology officer of X?" or "X's head of marketing is", the model replies with the chief executive's name. It happened on 9 or 10 of the 10 CEO facts at each of three checks. Teaching more ways of asking cannot fix it.

## The tools
Call a tool from a shell with:
"python" -m cramschool call <tool> @<arguments file>
The arguments file is a UTF-8 JSON object that you write in your work folder; leave it out for a tool without arguments. Every call prints JSON. Run one call at a time and read its result before the next.
- graft_status: the table as it is.
- graft_ask {"question", "chat"}: what the model says with and without the facts.
- graft_check_fact {"subject", "relation", "new_questions": [...], "other_questions": [...], "lookalike_companies": [...]}: asks the model your questions and tells you whether the answer came back on the new questions, and whether it leaked into the other questions. A question that ends with "?" or "." is sent as a chat message; any other text is a plain sentence opening that the model continues, so it must stop exactly where the answer would come next.
- graft_teach_not {"relation", "plain": [...], "chat": [...]}: teach a relation what it must not answer: questions about the same company that are close to the relation and are not it. Every wording has {s} exactly once where the company's name goes. A plain wording stops exactly where a name would come next; a chat wording is a question. A wording equal to one of the relation's own ways of asking is refused.
- graft_rebuild {"relation"}: build that relation's stored facts again so that they learn what you taught. About 2 minutes for 10 facts. Call it once only.
- graft_save {"path"}: save the table.

## Your job
1. See the failure yourself. Check each of the 10 CEO facts with graft_check_fact: 4 new questions that do ask for the chief executive, and 6 other questions that each ask for a different other job at the company. Mix plain openings and chat questions.
2. Teach the CEO relation what it must not answer, with graft_teach_not. Think about what separates a question for the chief executive from a question for any other job, and cover the range of other jobs and of ways of asking, not only the ones you saw fail: senior officers, department heads, specialists, site jobs; plain and chat; formal and casual. Never teach a wording that asks for the head of the whole company (who runs it, its boss, its managing director): the fact must go on answering those.
3. Call graft_rebuild {"relation": "ceo"} once.
4. Check all 10 CEO facts again, with questions you have neither used nor taught: other jobs that no wording you taught names, and new questions that do ask for the chief executive. A wording you taught proves nothing about itself.
5. Save the table to runs\must_not\editor\fixed.graft
6. Write report.json in your work folder: before and after, how many other-job questions got the chief executive's name (of how many asked) and on how many of the CEO questions the answer came back (of how many); every wording you taught; and what still leaks or was lost.

Reply with five lines at most: the numbers of your report, and what still leaks. Do not print the facts or the wordings.
